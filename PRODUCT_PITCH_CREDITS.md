# Read Assist — product pitch

This film introduces Read Assist through a connected story: making time for a growing reading list, listening while doing something else, understanding a word, and settling into comfortable reading.

The screen footage comes from the real Read Assist 0.3.2 Android emulator captures used for the existing walkthrough. The new edit enlarges authentic details and changes the composition to match the explanation. Recorded actions play at their original speed; editorial crops and pauses do not introduce app controls or simulated interactions. Dictionary footage shows manual search inside the reader. It does not demonstrate a selection gesture.

The English narration is generated separately in post-production using Kokoro v1.0 and the `af_heart` voice. The recorded app audio is muted to keep the explanation clear. This narration is not an example of the app's installed voice. The [voice credits](https://sainadh812.github.io/ReadFlow/PRODUCT_PITCH_VOICE_CREDITS.md) record sources, licenses and synthesis details. English captions and a downloadable soundtrack accompany the film. No music is added.

The final release preserves the approved 48.7-second edit, narration and captions. Only the editing-preview labels and delivery metadata were removed or updated for publication; source app recordings and the separate continuous playback video remain unchanged.

All sample reading text was created for the project. The adapted dictionary content visible in the recording remains licensed separately under CC BY-SA 4.0, with Wiktionary/Kaikki attribution. The film uses the project's Source Serif and Atkinson fonts. Original content, dictionary and font notices are retained in the [source media credits](https://sainadh812.github.io/ReadFlow/MEDIA_CREDITS.md).

Offline listening and definitions require an initial download of the corresponding packs. The video does not claim public Google Play availability. The continuous background-playback demonstration remains separately available, with its original footage and recorded speech.

The script, timeline and build reports record the edit, source hashes, audio processing and finished artifacts. Automatic transcription and media decoding checks support technical validation; they are not a human listening review.
